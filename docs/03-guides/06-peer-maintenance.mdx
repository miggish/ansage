---
title: Take one neighbor down
description: Disable, enable or reset a single BGP session, e.g. while its router is being worked on.
---

`ansage peer` acts on one neighbor and leaves the others alone. The neighbor is named by its ID
(the first column of `ansage show summary`), its address, or `address:port`.

```sh
ansage peer 10.0.0.1 disable --reason "r1 maintenance, ticket 4711"
ansage peer 10.0.0.1 enable
ansage peer 10.0.0.1 reset
```

| Command | NOTIFICATION sent | Afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| `disable [--reason TEXT]` | Cease / Administrative Shutdown, with TEXT (RFC 9003) | stays down until `enable` |
| `enable` | - | connects again |
| `reset` | Cease / Administrative Reset | reconnects |

The reason is shown on the router, for example in FRR's `show bgp neighbor` as the shutdown
message, and in ansage's own `ansage show peer`:

```txt
  Last down: 60.0s ago, administrative: cease (6/9) "maintenance"
```

<Callout type="info" title="6/9 instead of 6/2?">
With Graceful Restart and the N bit (RFC 8538) negotiated, ansage wraps the Cease in a Hard Reset
(6/9), so the neighbor withdraws our routes at once instead of keeping them as stale.
</Callout>

## Lifetime

A disabled neighbor stays disabled across a [reload](/docs/guides/reload) and enabled again by a
restart. `ansage_peer_disabled` is 1 while it is disabled.

## Disable vs. drain vs. remove

| Goal | Use |
|---|---|
| Move traffic off this whole server, sessions stay up | [`ansage drain`](/docs/guides/drain) |
| Close one session for a while | `ansage peer N disable` |
| Get rid of a neighbor | delete its `[[neighbor]]` and `ansage reload` (Cease / Peer De-configured) |
| Re-establish a stuck session | `ansage peer N reset` |
